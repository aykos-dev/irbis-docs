Mini-game · retention feature

Bu qayer? — Where is this?

A 3-minute daily geo-guess: look around a 360° view of a real place in Central Asia, drop a pin on the map, score by distance. It gives people a reason to open Irbis every day — on weekdays, in winter, in bad weather — and turns guessed places into real trips.

Core loop

Daily nudge

19:00 or the user's usual open time. Sleepy Irbis if the streak is at risk.

Look around

5 rounds of 360° views. Drag, zoom, look for clues.

Drop a pin

Closer = more points, up to 5,000 per round.

Reveal & reward

See the real place, small XP, badge progress, friends' scores.

Go there for real

"Save to my path" — first real check-in at a guessed place earns 2× XP.

Scoring

points = 5000 · e−d / k

d = distance from the true spot. k = pack scale: 30 km for Uzbekistan packs, 120 km for "All Central Asia", 8 km for city/park packs.

Retention levers

1

Same 5 places for everyone, reset at 00:00 Tashkent — people compare and talk about it.

2

Counts for the daily streak (lesson OR daily guess) — keeps streaks alive in winter.

3

Friends board & async duels — a challenge from a friend is a strong open trigger.

4

Weekly region packs + seasonal packs tied to collections like Autumn Colors.

5

Spoiler-free share card (score grid, no answers) — organic acquisition.

6

Your spots become rounds — "Your discovery was guessed 214 times" pulls creators back.

360° imagery source

Worldwide packs, decided: open-source first, Google only where needed.

1

Mapillary (primary, free) — global crowd-sourced 360° imagery, CC BY-SA. Register the app, show the Mapillary logo + link on every view.

2

Irbis community 360s — optional 360° step in Add a Spot (+20 XP); fills mountain gaps.

3

Google Street View (paid fallback) — only for curated rounds Mapillary lacks. Interactive views cost about $14 per 1,000 loads after 5,000 free a month ≈ $0.07 per 5-round daily.

Anti-cheat: strip EXIF/GPS, serve images via opaque IDs, score on the server, hide labels and signs that name the place.
